2023 · Virasoro TQFT

A new machine for an old question

Virasoro TQFT turns gluing into a computational language. A full definition of gravity still asks for more.

By now the same verbs keep returning: cut, fill, glue, transform, sum. We have used them for gauge connections, boundary surfaces, CFT states, and gravitational geometries. It is reasonable to ask whether a single mathematical machine can organize more of this work.

Virasoro TQFT is a modern proposal for such a machine. Its appeal is not simply that it says “gravity is topological.” That phrase was never enough to tell us what to calculate. The appeal is a much more concrete set of ingredients and operations.

Building blocks that know how to change channels

A conformal block isolates a particular symmetry-organized contribution to a CFT correlator or amplitude. It knows about an intermediate representation and the descendants attached to it. Different decompositions of a surface lead to different block bases.

Moving between those bases requires transformation kernels. These are not arbitrary changes of notation: their identities ensure compatibility among different ways of decomposing and reassembling a surface.

Work on Liouville theory, noncompact quantum groups, and quantum Teichmüller theory developed deep connections among these structures.[2][3] This is an important strand of the history that our short route only touches. The 2023 gravity proposal did not summon its mathematical ingredients from nowhere.

From identities to three-manifolds

In a topological quantum field theory, surfaces carry state spaces and three-dimensional cobordisms define operations on them. Gluing geometries corresponds to composing the associated operations. The promise is that a difficult-looking manifold can be handled by cutting it into tractable pieces, provided the rules are consistent.

Collier, Eberhardt, and Zhang proposed a precise AdS₃-gravity reformulation using a TQFT based on Teichmüller-space quantization and Virasoro data.[1] Their construction provides a systematic method for fixed-topology gravitational calculations, with nontrivial mapping-class-group considerations in relating the TQFT to gravity.

This is not an ordinary finite-state topological theory, and the paper explicitly distinguishes it from standard \(SL(2,\mathbb R)\) Chern–Simons theory. The resemblance to the early CS story is real; replacing one name with the other would lose the point.

What “solving” can mean

There is a substantial difference between an algorithm for an amplitude at a chosen topology and a complete prescription for the full gravitational path integral.

Once an amplitude is available, we can still ask which topologies to include, how to weight them, whether the resulting sum converges or needs a prescription, and how off-shell contributions fit into the definition. We can also ask what exact boundary object reproduces the complete answer.

These questions are not complaints about a useful machine. A machine can solve an important class of calculations while leaving the choice of what to calculate open. Conflating the two would make it harder to appreciate the actual achievement.

Think of obtaining a powerful grammar for assembling sentences. The grammar is a real advance. It does not by itself decide which book to write, how to sum over every possible book, or whether that sum defines the object you intended.

The route comes back to its beginning

Our first article asked what survives when local gravitational waves disappear. We now have a much richer answer: global geometry, boundary symmetry, black-hole states, operator consistency, and the problem of a complete quantum definition.

The CS observation taught us to reorganize the variables. BTZ forced us to count states. Modular sums taught us that symmetry alone does not guarantee a sensible spectrum. Higher genus sharpened the meaning of consistency. Wormholes widened the possible boundary target. Virasoro TQFT supplies new ways to compute the objects that these questions demand.

This selected route ends with a method and a question, rather than a claim that the subject is finished. Given all this structure, what precisely is the quantum object whose amplitudes we are calculating?

That question is the reason to keep the notebook open.
